# Style kit

Clear, specific plain English that a general reader understands the first time. Adapted from the GOV.UK A to Z style guide under the Open Government Licence v3.0.

## Rules

| ID | Title | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| gv-ampersand | Use 'and', not an ampersand | Use 'and' rather than '&', unless it is part of a logo or a registered company name. |
| gv-in-order-to | Remove 'in order to' | 'In order to' is usually not needed. Do not use it. |
| gv-please | Avoid 'please' | There is usually no need to say 'please' or 'please note'. |
| gv-ordinal-date | No ordinal numbers in dates | Do not use ordinal numbers for the day: 2 June, not 2nd June. |
| gv-range-hyphen | Use 'to' in ranges | Use 'to' in number and date ranges, not hyphens or dashes: 500 to 900, tax year 2011 to 2012. Tables are the exception. |
| gv-eg-etc-ie | Avoid eg, etc and ie | Use 'for example', 'such as', 'like' or 'including' instead of eg and etc. Rewrite to avoid ie, or use 'meaning' or 'that is'. |
| gv-contraction | Avoid negative and complex contractions | Avoid negative contractions such as can't and don't, and complex ones such as should've, could've and would've. |
| gv-click | Say 'select', not 'click' | Do not use 'click' when talking about user interfaces, because not all users click. Use 'select'. 'Right-click' is allowed when it is really needed. |
| gv-money-decimals | No decimals on whole pounds | Do not use decimals unless pence are included: £75.50, but not £75.00. |
| gv-thousands-comma | Comma in numbers over 999 | For numerals over 999, insert a comma for clarity: 9,000. Years, references and codes are not counted. |
| gv-per-cent | Use the % sign | Use a % sign for percentages: 50%. |
| gv-small-numbers | Use numerals for 2 to 9 | Write numbers in numerals, including 2 to 9, except in common expressions such as 'one or two of them' and when a number starts a sentence. |
| gv-age-groups | Say who is included by age | Avoid 'the over 50s' or 'under-18s'. Make clear who is included: 'aged 50 years and over', 'aged 17 and under'. |
| gv-today-date | Give the date with 'today' | When referring to today, as in a news article, include the date: 'announced today (14 June 2012)'. |
| gv-sentence-length | Keep sentences short | Do not use long sentences. Check sentences with more than 25 words to see whether you can split them. |
| gv-active-voice | Use the active voice | Use the active voice rather than the passive voice, so content is concise and clear. Good: "We will publish the results in May." Bad: "The results will be published in May." |
| gv-abbreviations | Explain abbreviations on first use | The first time you use an abbreviation on a page, explain it in full. Well-known ones such as BBC, CCTV, DIY, DVLA, EU, GP, HTML, ID, NHS, UK and VAT need no explanation. Good: "Personal Independence Payment (PIP)" Bad: "Claim PIP using the form." |
| gv-metaphor | Avoid metaphors | Avoid metaphors, because they do not say what you mean and slow people down. Be open and specific about what is actually happening. Good: "This will create more jobs." Bad: "This will drive growth across the sector." |
| gv-uk-english | Use UK English | Use UK spelling and grammar: organise, not organize; modelling, not modeling; fill in a form, not fill out a form. American proper nouns keep their spelling. |
| gv-plain-english | Write in plain English | Write in plain English that the audience will understand. Replace official jargon and vague abstract phrasing with specific, everyday words. Good: "We will give councils more money for housing." Bad: "We will enhance local capacity for housing outcomes." |
